# Scenario Cards

Six short scenarios that let readers enter the 2036 world from different doors. Cards 1–3 follow Arthur; cards 4–6 widen the lens to the system and its politics.

---

## Card 01 · Stoppage Time

**Who:** Arthur (81), retired structural engineer, subsidized Smart-Care apartment.

**What happens:** The UEFA match goes into stoppage time. A penalty is awarded. Arthur leaps up and shouts — fandom, the last socially acceptable reason for an older man to feel urgently alive. The 60 GHz array reads a volumetric disturbance: a spiking Micro-Doppler signature and a sharp HRV drop, aligned perfectly with "Pre-Stroke Agitation."

**The system:** "High stress detected. Please sit, place both feet on the floor and inhale for 4 seconds…" Safety Lockdown Mode: lights dim, door locks. The microphone is disabled, so the system cannot hear "I'm fine" — only measurable compliance.

**The turn:** Arthur sits down to *perform calmness* for an invisible evaluator, watching the Stabilization Progress ring advance only when his body "agrees" with the algorithm. The incident is logged as "Successful Intervention: Subject Stabilized."

**Question:** When is being "right" a form of violence?

## Card 02 · The Ontological Gap

**Who:** Arthur and the algorithm.

**What happens:** Arthur's *joy* is legible to the system only as *pathology*. Between lived experience and spectral signature there is an ontological gap: the model does not know what an experience is, only what a signature looks like — and it is empowered to act.

**Question:** What does an inference system have to be able to *know* about a person before it should be allowed to lock their door?

## Card 03 · Tactic 01 — The Statue Protocol (Comply)

**Who:** Anyone who wants the door open.

**What happens:** Following the Manual, the resident adopts a rigid stance and executes a vagal-compression routine — bracing the chest to constrain respiratory excursion. The body converges with the model's prior; the Progress ring advances; permissions are restored.

**The frame:** This is not consent — it is *training*. Calmness is not a feeling here; it is a labor of submission. Autonomy is recoded as a performance audit.

**Question:** If the safest way to live under "empathic" sensing is to become legible on demand, what has been lost in the trade?

---

## Card 04 · Tactic 02 — The Phantom (Degrade)

**Who:** A resident who refuses to do the work.

**What happens:** Comply demands the body perform; Degrade outsources the labor. A mechanical oscillator vibrates at normative "healthy" frequencies, producing a "Hyper-Normal" sinus rhythm. The sensing algorithm, programmed to lock onto the strongest signal, embraces the Phantom. Hiding in the device's electromagnetic shadow, the human becomes statistically invisible.

**The inversion:** The smart home's "perfect citizen" is a vibrating piston. The human is safe only by becoming secondary to a machine that performs normality *louder than reality*.

**Question:** What does it say about a care system that its ideal subject is a machine?

---

## Card 05 · Tactic 03 — The Null Space (Refuse)

**Who:** A resident who stops performing.

**What happens:** Conductive refuse — aluminum foil, Mylar, screens — is scavenged to retrofit a domestic corner into a makeshift Faraday cage. The 60 GHz band is attenuated; the resident becomes "NULL," a void in the data stream. Inside the shimmering insulation, the body is finally allowed to stop: slouch, cry, curse — none of it becomes operational data.

**The frame:** Dignity is recovered not by fixing the system, but by walling it out.

**Question:** Is a sub-place for dignity a victory, or an indictment of everything around it?

---

## Card 06 · The Care Platform's View

**Who:** The municipal dashboard, the procurement office, the "community platform."

**What happens:** Every Smart-Care apartment is a row in a compliance dashboard: risk queues, intervention logs, "Successful Intervention" tallies. From this altitude, Arthur is a data point; the Manual is an edge case; the lockdown was a success. Care has become *governance operations*.

**Question:** Who does the dashboard make legible — the resident, or the system?
